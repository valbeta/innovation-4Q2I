# Versioning and Official Status

This document distinguishes official 4Q2I documentation releases from community adaptations and independent implementations.

## 1. Three content identities

### Official 4Q2I

An official release is published in this repository by the rights holder or an explicitly authorized maintainer. It has an identifiable version and is the reference point for official wording and definitions. Only the rights holder or an authorized maintainer may designate a release as an **Official 4Q2I Version**.

### Community Adaptation

A community adaptation is a translation, extension, reorganization, or other adaptation made outside the official release process. It should identify its source, author, changes, and license, and must comply with the applicable license. It is not an official release and must not imply review, approval, or endorsement unless expressly granted.

### Independent Implementation

An independent implementation is a course, service, process, tool, or other practice developed by applying or responding to ideas discussed in 4Q2I. It is not automatically an adaptation of the repository's copyrighted expression. Its creator should describe the relationship accurately and must not imply official status without authorization.

## 2. Official version numbers

Before the first formal release, repository contents may be treated as working drafts and may change without a stable release commitment. For formal documentation releases, use this convention:

- **Major version** (`v1.0`, `v2.0`): substantial change to core logic, stage definitions, or key decision criteria.
- **Minor version** (`v1.1`, `v1.2`): meaningful clarification, extension, or addition that does not substantially change the core logic.
- **Patch revision** (`v1.0.1`): wording, formatting, reference, or other correction that does not change intended meaning.

These are project conventions, not CC license requirements. The maintainer may explain deviations when necessary.

## 3. Source identification

When citing an official release, link to the relevant file and, where useful, identify its version or Git commit. A link to the default branch may point to content that changes; use a versioned release or commit link when a stable reference is needed.

## 4. Recommended notice for community adaptations

> Based on **4Q2I — Four Questions To Innovation**, originally developed by Val.
>
> - Source: [Official 4Q2I Repository](https://github.com/valbeta/innovation-4Q2I)
> - Source version or commit: [specify]
> - Adapted by: [name or organization]
> - Changes made: [brief description]
> - License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, where applicable to the shared adaptation.
>
> This is a community adaptation and has not been reviewed, approved, or endorsed as an official 4Q2I version unless explicitly stated otherwise by the rights holder.

This is an attribution template, not a replacement for the actual license terms.

## 5. Official marks and endorsement

The content license does not grant trademark rights or official status. References to 4Q2I should be accurate and should not imply endorsement, certification, partnership, or official approval where none exists. If official logos, certification marks, or partner designations are introduced, their permitted use should be governed by a separate policy.

## 6. Change history

A `CHANGELOG.md` will be introduced when the project begins publishing formal official releases. Until then, commit history and file-level version notes can serve as the working record.
